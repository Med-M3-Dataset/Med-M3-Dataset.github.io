Background

DA-MIVQA extends the NLPCC 2023–2025 medical instructional video question answering tasks by introducing difficulty-aware evaluation. Instead of only measuring overall performance, the new benchmark explicitly distinguishes between simple questions, which can be answered mainly through direct subtitle-aligned evidence, and complex questions, which require integrating subtitles, visual demonstrations, and procedural context. This design aims to better evaluate robustness in realistic medical education and emergency knowledge access scenarios.

Task Overview

Three Shared Task Tracks

1

Track 1: DA-TAGSV

Given a medical question and a single untrimmed medical instructional video, the system must locate the temporal answer span within the video.

Evaluation Metrics

mIoU, R@1 with IoU = 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7, reported on simple, complex, and overall subsets.

2

Track 2: DA-VCR

Given a medical question and a large video corpus, the system must retrieve the most relevant instructional video.

Evaluation Metrics

R@1, R@10, R@50, and MRR, reported on simple, complex, and overall subsets.

3

Track 3: DA-TAGVC

Given a medical question and a large video corpus, the system must retrieve the target video and then localize the answer span inside it.

Evaluation Metrics

R@1|mIoU, R@10|mIoU, R@50|mIoU, and Average, reported on simple, complex, and overall subsets.

Dataset

Re-annotation of NLPCC 2023–2025 Video Resources

DA-MIVQA is built through systematic re-annotation of the video resources used in NLPCC 2023, 2024, and 2025. Instead of creating an entirely new corpus, the benchmark reuses high-quality medical instructional videos and enriches them with difficulty-aware labels.

The videos cover first aid, emergency response, rehabilitation, nursing practice, home care, and general medical education. Each sample includes a question, a unique temporal answer span, and structured metadata for retrieval and localization.

Simple / Complex Labels

Meaning of the New Difficulty Annotation

The simple/complex distinction reflects the main evidence source required to answer a question. Simple questions can often be answered mainly through subtitle-aligned textual cues or other explicit single-source evidence. Complex questions cannot be reliably solved from subtitles alone and instead require multimodal integration, such as visual actions, tool use, posture, and procedural transitions across time.

Simple
Text-dominant matching, direct evidence, single-source cues.
Complex
Multimodal procedural understanding, temporal and visual evidence integration.
